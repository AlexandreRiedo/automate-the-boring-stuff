The old lighthouse had not been staffed for thirty years, but the light
still turned every night. Nobody in the village could say who kept the
mechanism oiled, and nobody asked too loudly.

Maren walked the cliff path most mornings, counting the gulls that nested
in the rock face below. There were fewer of them each spring, and she had
started writing the numbers in a notebook she kept in her coat pocket.

On the third of April she found a brass key wedged into a crack near the
lighthouse door. It was warm, which made no sense at all, because the sun
had not been out for two days.
